# Portfolio Entry: Best Practice "User Experience" **UX Basics Every Website Needs** *Niklas, apprentice application developer (EFZ) at SBB, documenting his learning progress in module M293.* ## What is User Experience (UX)? User Experience describes how a person feels when using a website or application, and how easily they achieve their goal. It's not just about looks (design), but above all about whether the site is understandable, efficient, and pleasant to use. ## Key Best Practices **1. Clear Navigation** Users must always know where they are and how to go back or forward. Consistent menu placement (e.g. always in the same spot) reduces cognitive load. **2. Consistency** Colors, fonts, button styles, and spacing should be used uniformly across the whole site. This builds trust and makes the interface predictable. **3. Fast Loading Times** Studies show users often abandon a page after just 2-3 seconds of loading time. Images should be compressed and code kept as lean as possible. **4. Readability** Sufficient contrast between text and background, a sensible font size (at least 16px for body text), and enough line spacing make reading easier. **5. Mobile First / Responsive Design** Since a large share of users access sites via smartphone, the page must work and look good on every screen size. **6. Clear Calls-to-Action** Buttons like "Order now" or "Contact us" should stand out visually and be worded clearly, so the user knows exactly what to do next. **7. Error Prevention and Handling** Forms should validate input directly and show understandable error messages (e.g. "Invalid email address" instead of just "Error"). **8. Less is More (Reduction)** Too much information or too many elements at once overwhelm users. Important content should be prioritized and the rest trimmed down. ## Personal Conclusion For me, UX is mainly a question of empathy: you have to put yourself in the shoes of users who are often seeing the site for the first time and have no technical background. In my own projects, I'll pay closer attention to consistency and clear calls-to-action going forward, since I've handled these more instinctively than deliberately so far. I also intend to think about loading times and form validation from the start, instead of optimizing them only at the end.
